How South African Businesses Are Digitising in 2024

January 2025 · 8 min read · By Mapengo Innovations

The SA Digital Landscape in 2024

South Africa has seen accelerated digital adoption in the years following the pandemic. Over 60% of SMEs have now invested in some form of digital presence — whether that's a website, social media storefront, or a cloud-based business system. Load-shedding has, paradoxically, pushed many businesses toward cloud-first solutions that don't depend on local infrastructure remaining online. And mobile-first is no longer optional: over 70% of SA internet traffic is mobile, and that number continues to climb.

What's Actually Working

E-commerce with PayFast and Yoco integrations is booming. Businesses using mobile-responsive, fast-loading websites report 2–3× higher engagement than their desktop-first counterparts. WhatsApp Business integrations for customer service have dramatically reduced support costs for SMEs — customers get answers instantly without expensive call centres.

Cloud-based accounting and stock management platforms like Sage and Xero are being adopted by businesses that previously relied entirely on spreadsheets. The combination of accessibility, automatic backups and load-shedding resilience makes cloud software the obvious choice for SA businesses in 2024.

The Biggest Obstacles to Digitisation

Load-shedding remains a critical concern — digital products must be designed with offline capability or cloud resilience in mind. A platform that goes down every time the power goes out quickly loses user trust.

High data costs mean that heavy websites with unoptimised images and bloated JavaScript bundles lose customers before they ever see the product. Trust in online payments is growing but remains a barrier — businesses need visible security signals like SSL certificates, recognised payment logos, and genuine customer reviews.

Skills gaps in digital literacy remain a significant challenge, particularly for businesses in peri-urban and rural areas where access to training and support is limited.

The SA-Specific Playbook

Based on what we've seen across dozens of SA digitisation projects, here's what actually works:

  1. Start mobile — build for smartphones first. Your desktop experience can follow.
  2. Optimise aggressively for data-light experiences — compress images, remove unused JavaScript, and treat every kilobyte as if your customers are paying for it.
  3. Integrate SA payment gateways from day one — PayFast, Yoco and Peach Payments are trusted by SA consumers.
  4. Design for offline or intermittent connectivity — use service workers and progressive web app techniques where appropriate.
  5. Use local hosting or CDNs with SA edge nodes — AWS Cape Town (af-south-1) and local CDNs dramatically improve load times for SA users.
  6. Build trust signals prominently — SA consumers are understandably cautious online. Make security visible and obvious.

What 2025 Holds

We expect 2025 to bring accelerated adoption of AI-assisted customer service — chatbots, automated booking systems and intelligent routing are already proving ROI for early adopters. Mobile payments will continue to grow, with QR-code-based payments becoming standard. Most importantly, the businesses that invest in digital infrastructure now will have a significant competitive advantage over those that delay. The gap between digitally-enabled businesses and those still operating manually is widening every quarter.
