Choosing the Right Tech Stack for Your SA Business

November 2024 · 7 min read · By Mapengo Innovations

Why Stack Choice Matters More in SA

Choosing a tech stack is never purely a technical decision. In South Africa it's also a business decision with long-term implications. The local developer talent pool, support availability, hosting costs priced in ZAR, and the practical difficulty of maintaining niche technology years down the line — all of these factors matter enormously.

A stack that's popular in San Francisco might leave a Johannesburg SME unable to find maintenance developers at a reasonable rate. A framework built for massive scale might add R50K/month in infrastructure costs before the business is ready for that scale. The right stack for a South African business is the one that balances capability, local talent availability, infrastructure cost and long-term maintainability.

The Front-End: React, Next.js and Vue.js

React dominates the SA developer market. If you want to be able to hire, hand off or get external support for your frontend, React is the safest choice. The talent pool is deep and growing.

Next.js (built on React) is excellent for SEO-critical sites and e-commerce — server-side rendering means faster initial page loads and better Google indexing, both crucial for SA businesses competing for organic traffic.

Vue.js is a good alternative if your team has strong Vue skills — it's learnable and has a decent SA developer base. Avoid overly niche frameworks like Svelte or Solid for commercial projects unless your team is expert in them. The support and talent implications are significant.

The Back-End: Node.js, Python and PHP

Node.js has a strong ecosystem in SA and is an excellent choice for APIs, real-time features and businesses that want a JavaScript-everywhere stack. The event-driven architecture handles concurrent connections efficiently — important for high-traffic SA applications.

Python is excellent for data-heavy applications, machine learning integrations, and complex business logic. The SA talent pool for Python is strong, particularly among developers with university backgrounds. Django and FastAPI are both solid framework choices.

PHP/Laravel remains entirely viable for SMEs with budget constraints. It's battle-tested, hosting is cheap, and there are many SA developers who know it well. Avoid Java for SME projects — the operational overhead, hosting costs and developer rates create a cost structure that rarely makes sense below R10M ARR.

Databases: SQL vs NoSQL in the SA Context

PostgreSQL is our recommended default for transactional data — financial records, user accounts, order management, inventory. It's rock-solid, ACID compliant, and handles relational data with consistency that matters for SA businesses dealing with payments and compliance.

MongoDB makes sense for flexible or document-oriented data — content management, user preferences, logging, IoT data from agricultural systems. Don't use it for financial data where ACID compliance matters.

Avoid over-engineering with microservices, event sourcing, or CQRS architectures until you genuinely need that scale. Most SA businesses at the SME stage need a well-built monolith, not a distributed system.

Hosting: Local vs Global

AWS has a Cape Town region (af-south-1) — this is an excellent choice for production SA applications. Latency for SA users is dramatically better than hosting in Ireland or Frankfurt, and your data stays in South Africa, simplifying POPIA compliance.

Vercel and Netlify are great choices for frontend deployments — they have CDN edge nodes in SA and make deployment simple. For a Next.js or React app, Vercel is often the fastest path to production.

Avoid cheap shared hosting for production business applications. The cost savings are illusory — poor performance costs you customers, and shared hosts are the first to have outages during load-shedding-related power events. Budget properly for hosting: R500–R2,000/month for a small production app is realistic and worth every rand.

The SA Payment Stack

PayFast is the most widely used SA payment gateway and the default choice for most e-commerce projects. It supports cards, EFT, instant EFT, and QR codes, and SA consumers recognise and trust it.

Yoco is excellent for businesses that need both in-person (POS terminal) and online payment capability in a unified system. The API is clean and the onboarding is fast.

Peach Payments is the right choice for enterprise clients that need advanced features, multiple currencies, or complex payment flows. Avoid Stripe unless you have significant international customer volumes — the ZAR conversion fees and setup friction add up quickly for businesses primarily serving SA customers.
