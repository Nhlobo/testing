E-Commerce in South Africa: What Actually Works

March 2025 · 8 min read · By Mapengo Innovations

The SA E-Commerce Opportunity

South African e-commerce is growing at double-digit rates year on year, yet online retail still accounts for a small fraction of total retail spend compared to developed markets. This gap is the opportunity. The early movers who build excellent SA-native online stores now will capture significant market share as the broader shift to online shopping accelerates over the next decade.

The key insight is that SA e-commerce success requires a different playbook to what works in the US or UK. The payment landscape is different, the logistics challenges are real, trust is harder to earn, and the mobile experience is everything. Getting these right from the outset separates the businesses that win from those that fail to gain traction.

Payment Gateways: The SA-Native Stack

PayFast is the bedrock of SA e-commerce. It supports credit/debit cards, instant EFT, Masterpass, SnapScan and SCode. SA consumers know and trust the PayFast logo — displaying it on your checkout page is a conversion signal. For most stores, PayFast should be your primary gateway.

Yoco is excellent for omnichannel businesses that sell both online and in-person — the unified dashboard simplifies reconciliation and reporting. Their developer API is clean and documentation is solid.

Ozow (formerly i-Pay) enables instant EFT payments without card details — valuable for the large SA segment that is card-absent or prefers not to enter card details online. Ozow conversions are often 15–25% higher for EFT-preferred customers.

Avoid Stripe as a primary SA gateway. ZAR conversion fees, USD pricing, and reduced trust from SA consumers add unnecessary friction and cost. Reserve Stripe for international transactions if your business has a genuine need.

Building Trust: The SA Shopper's Mindset

Online shopping fraud has made SA consumers cautious. Trust signals are not optional — they are conversion-critical. Every e-commerce store selling to SA customers must visibly display:

  • SSL certificate — the padlock must be present. Without HTTPS, modern browsers actively warn users. No SA consumer will enter card details on an HTTP site.
  • Recognised payment logos — PayFast, Visa, Mastercard, and EFT logos on the checkout page build confidence.
  • Real contact details — a physical address (even a registered business address), phone number, and email address. SA consumers want to know they can reach a real business.
  • Returns and refund policy — clear, prominent, and fair. The Consumer Protection Act gives SA shoppers strong rights; make it easy for them to find your policy.
  • Customer reviews — authentic, verified reviews drive conversion. Integrate with Hello Peter or Google Reviews, or display testimonials with real names and locations.

Social proof matters enormously in SA — WhatsApp screenshots of positive customer feedback, Facebook reviews, and word-of-mouth referrals remain powerful conversion drivers, particularly in community and township markets.

Logistics: The Last-Mile Challenge

Delivery is where many SA e-commerce ventures struggle. Last-mile logistics outside major metros can be unreliable, expensive, and slow. Managing customer expectations around delivery time is as important as the delivery itself.

The major SA courier integrations worth building are The Courier Guy, Aramex, and Fastway (Evri) for nationwide coverage. For Gauteng and Western Cape, Pargo (click-and-collect) provides a valuable option for customers who prefer to collect at a local pick-up point — it solves the "no one home" problem and gives cost-conscious customers a cheaper delivery option.

Multi-carrier rate quoting at checkout — showing the customer their actual delivery cost before they commit — significantly reduces cart abandonment. Hidden shipping costs are the number-one reason SA online shoppers abandon carts at checkout.

Platform Choice: Custom vs WooCommerce vs Shopify

WooCommerce (WordPress) remains the dominant e-commerce platform in SA by install count. It's highly flexible, well-supported locally, and integrates with all major SA payment gateways. For businesses with complex product catalogues, variable pricing, or B2B needs, WooCommerce provides the flexibility that hosted platforms cannot.

Shopify has grown its SA presence considerably. It's excellent for straightforward product catalogues with standard checkout flows, and its app ecosystem is mature. The main limitation is cost — USD pricing for apps and transaction fees hurts SA businesses operating in ZAR margins.

Custom-built e-commerce (Next.js + headless commerce, for example) makes sense only for businesses with specific requirements that platforms cannot accommodate — unusual product configuration, complex B2B workflows, or very high traffic that demands CDN-delivered static pages. For most SA retail businesses, WooCommerce or Shopify will outperform a custom build in time-to-market and cost.

Mobile Checkout Optimisation

Over 65% of SA e-commerce traffic is mobile, but mobile conversion rates are typically 30–40% lower than desktop. The gap is almost entirely attributable to checkout friction. Reduce form fields to the minimum viable set, implement address autocomplete, ensure touch targets are large enough for thumbs, and never force account creation before checkout. Guest checkout is not optional for SA consumers — it's expected.
