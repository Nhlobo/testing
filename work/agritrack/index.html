The Challenge

A consortium of commercial farms in the Western Cape and Limpopo was losing significant money through poor inventory tracking, duplicate supplier orders, and manual crop-cycle recording on paper. Stock wastage averaged 18% per season — seed, fertiliser and chemicals expiring unused or over-ordered without visibility.

There was no visibility into input costs versus yield, making it impossible to identify which crops and practices were most profitable across the different farms and climatic zones. The farmers were flying blind financially, and with margins already tight, this was an existential challenge.

Our Solution

We built a Django-based web application with a PostgreSQL database, hosted on AWS in the Cape Town region (af-south-1). The system includes an inventory module with barcode scanning, a crop cycle tracker with input cost recording, a supplier portal for purchase orders, and a financial reporting module with profitability analysis by crop and farm.

Each farm operates as a separate tenant with role-based access: farm managers see their full operation, field supervisors manage their assigned sections, and the accounts team accesses financial reporting across the consortium. Celery handles background tasks like automated reorder alerts and report generation.

An offline-sync feature was critical — field workers in remote sections of the farm can capture inventory and crop data without connectivity, with the app syncing automatically when back in range. This was essential for the Limpopo farms with patchy cellular coverage.
