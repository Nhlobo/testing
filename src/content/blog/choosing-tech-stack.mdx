One of the most common questions we get from South African businesses building their first digital product: "What technology should we use?"

The honest answer: it depends. But let me give you a framework to decide, and our recommendations based on years of building for SA businesses.

## The Questions to Ask First

Before choosing a technology, answer these:

1. **What is the primary use case?** (E-commerce, internal tool, customer-facing app, marketplace)
2. **Who will maintain it long term?** (Your in-house team, outsourced, us)
3. **What is your budget for development and hosting?**
4. **Do you need offline capabilities?** (Critical in SA for load shedding and patchy connectivity)
5. **How fast do you need to launch?** (MVP vs polished v1)

## Our Recommended Stack by Use Case

### For Business Websites & Landing Pages
**Astro + TypeScript**

Why: Generates static HTML for near-instant load times. Perfect for SEO. Extremely low hosting costs (even free on GitHub Pages). Supports React components when needed.

Best for: Service businesses, professional services, informational sites

### For Web Applications & Portals
**Next.js + TypeScript + PostgreSQL**

Why: React with server-side rendering. Excellent developer ecosystem. Easily deployed on Vercel or Render. TypeScript catches bugs before they reach production.

Best for: Dashboards, portals, SaaS applications, customer platforms

### For E-Commerce
**Next.js (headless) + Shopify OR WooCommerce (for smaller stores)**

Why: Shopify handles payments, inventory, and checkout reliably. A Next.js frontend gives you full design control and performance. For smaller budgets, WooCommerce works well with proper hosting.

Best for: Online retail, product catalogues with purchasing

### For Mobile Apps
**React Native**

Why: One codebase for iOS and Android. Leverages React knowledge if you already have web developers. Strong ecosystem. Works well in low-bandwidth SA environments with proper architecture.

Best for: Customer-facing apps, field worker tools, logistics apps

### For APIs & Backend Services
**Node.js (NestJS) OR Python (FastAPI)**

Why: Node.js shares language with your frontend team (TypeScript). FastAPI is excellent for data-heavy applications and ML integration. Both have strong SA developer communities.

## What to Avoid (And Why)

### PHP/WordPress for Complex Applications
WordPress is fine for simple sites, but building complex applications on it leads to maintenance nightmares. The plugin ecosystem becomes a security and performance liability.

### Outdated Frameworks
AngularJS (not Angular 2+), Backbone.js, CoffeeScript — avoid these. Recruiting developers is harder and the ecosystem is dying.

### Overly Complex Architecture Too Early
Microservices, Kubernetes, message queues — these are solutions to problems you probably do not have yet. Start simple. A monolith that works beats a distributed system that is difficult to maintain.

## The South Africa Factor

A few considerations specific to SA:

**Load shedding resilience**: Choose architectures with offline capabilities. Progressive Web Apps with service workers. Local data caching. Graceful degradation when connectivity fails.

**Data efficiency**: Optimise payload sizes. Avoid heavy client-side JavaScript frameworks for simple content sites. Every unnecessary kilobyte is money from your users' data budget.

**Local developer availability**: Choosing React and Node.js means you can hire locally. Obscure frameworks make hiring very difficult.

**Cost**: Rand-denominated businesses should favour open-source stacks. SaaS subscriptions in USD are expensive when R/$ rates fluctuate.
